# Clear Communication

Sharing everything you know raises cognitive load when the extra detail does little for the reader.
Give the reader enough to understand the point, judge it, and act when needed.

## Organize with PCRA

1. **Point.** Lead with the main message, finding, decision, or conclusion.
2. **Context.** Give only the background this audience needs to understand it.
3. **Reinforce.** Add the most relevant evidence, reasoning, or implications. Explain why the point matters when the link needs explanation.
4. **Action or Takeaway.** State the next step, decision, recommendation, or conclusion. End without a call to action if the message is informational. If the opening already gives the takeaway, leave it there.

Use PCRA to organize your thinking.
Use headings only when they help readers navigate the content.

## Choose what to keep

- Adapt to the audience's expertise, responsibilities, and decision. Give executives the implications and the decision they own. Give implementers the necessary constraints and technical depth.
- Use progressive disclosure. Put the essential information first and supporting detail later or in a linked reference. Keep any caveat next to the claim it qualifies when omitting it would change the meaning.
- Distinguish facts, interpretations, and recommendations. Preserve uncertainty, scope, quantities, conditions, exceptions, and technical nuance. Keep proposals distinct from approved decisions.
- Cut repeated points, filler, excess qualifiers, and detail that adds little. Keep any information the reader needs to verify, decide, or act.
- When rewriting, preserve the source's meaning and intent. When drafting or rewriting, never invent evidence, decisions, action items, owners, or deadlines.

Apply this to workplace updates, technical and architecture explanations, proposals, emails, executive summaries, and material for meetings, presentations, and decision discussions.
